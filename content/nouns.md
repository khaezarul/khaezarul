Nouns in Kálasítü are fairly simple. There are no cases, grammatical gender, or irregular declensions to memorize.

There are both definite and indefinite articles, and the plural is marked on the article rather than the noun itself. These articles come after the noun, and before any adjectives or numbers. The system can be summarized in this table:

|       | singular | plural | collective |
| ----- | -------- | ------ | ---------- |
| indefinite | a        | ni     | nisi       |
| definite   | wa       | kóu    | kóüsi      |

You might notice that apart from singular and plural, there is also the collective. This could be seen as meaning "(noun) and such". Kálasítü has many specific words, but often lacks more general ones that we English speakers are used to. For example, there is no word for "fruit", but there are words for many individual fruits, such as raspberries and plums. If one wanted to refer to "fruit", one could say "plum" with the collective article, making it into "plums and such". This is heavily dependent on context; the example of plums could also mean "fruits that come from a tree", "things with a similar color to plums", and so on.

Compounds can be formed by simply putting one noun before the other. However, this is done in the opposite order than how it works in English. In English, the last word in the compound determines what kind of thing it is: a "bedbug" is a type of bug (not a type of bed) and a "cellar door" is a type of door (not a type of cellar). In Kálasítü, it is the first word in the compound that determines this. Thus a "bedbug", if translated literally, would be interpreted as a bed made for bugs, or a bed that is full of bugs.
